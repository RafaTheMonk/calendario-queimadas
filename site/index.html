The story in 4 steps

New here? This is what the numbers below mean.

  1. 1

    The jump that never happened

    In 2012, hotspots in Bahia jumped from 26,477 to 210,941. Almost all of that jump was not more fire: the new satellite (VIIRS) sees smaller fires and counts about 6 times more than the old one (MODIS).

  2. 2

    One ruler for both

    For each calendar month we learned how many VIIRS hotspots match one MODIS hotspot (from 9.6 in the rainy season to 5.7 in the dry season) and converted 2003 to 2011 to the new scale.

  3. 3

    How to read the app

    In the calendar, each square is a month and the color shows how many hotspots there were; the other tab shows if the month was above or below normal. The Now panel compares the last 7 days with the normal for this month.

  4. 4

    Why trust it

    Learning only from 2012 to 2019, the monthly error on 2020 to 2023 is 7.2%. Against INPE, the 2012 jump disappears: the ratio between the two series changes only 11% across 2012, against a 6.2-fold jump without the conversion.

Area of interest

Tap the map to choose a point, draw the outline of an area, use your location or go back to the whole of Bahia. In this version, the processed data covers Bahia, Brazil.

Tap the map to mark the corners (a municipality, a reserve, a farm). With 3 points or more, click "Finish area". The grid cells whose center falls inside the outline are included.

Hotspots on the map (NASA FIRMS)

Your location is only requested when you click "Use my location". Your coordinate is not sent: for the last days, the site only sends the list of grid cells (tens of km). Opening the map downloads the OpenStreetMap tiles of the region. To show hotspots on the map, the site only sends the mode and the date to our function.
